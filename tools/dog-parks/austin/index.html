Park prep for Austin weather

  • Pavement check first. Getting to the park often means sidewalks: on warm days do the 7-second pavement test before you set out. Asphalt can run 40 to 60 degrees hotter than the air.
  • August is Austin's hottest month (average high 97.8°F), which means dangerous heat for park time. Bring water for the park; shared bowls are a gamble.
  • Winter park trips need paw care. Austin averages 0.2″ of snow, and the ice-melt salt that follows burns pads. Rinse paws after winter visits, or use booties.
  • After rain, expect mud and check the live verdict above before you drive over: a storm cell can close your window fast. Post-rain zoomies are real; towel accordingly.
  • Coldest stretch: January lows average 41.8°F in Austin. Below about 45°F, small and thin-coated dogs want a coat even for park time.

Austin dog park questions

OpenStreetMap currently maps 60 dog parks within about 12 miles of central Austin. The closest to the city center is Dog park (unnamed on OpenStreetMap), about 0.6 miles out. Community data grows all the time, so a missing spot just means nobody has mapped it yet.
Austin's hottest month is August with average highs of 97.8 degrees. Above 82 degrees most dogs should keep outdoor time short and shaded, and above 90 degrees skip it entirely. The live verdict on this page applies those same bands to right-now conditions.
Fencing details are not recorded for Austin's mapped parks yet, which does not mean they are unfenced. Check the park's official page or visit at a quiet hour first.
Water and a bowl, waste bags, and a towel in the wetter months. In summer go early or late and test the pavement with the back of your hand for 7 seconds first. In winter, with Austin averaging 0.2 inches of snow, rinse paws after to clear ice-melt salt.

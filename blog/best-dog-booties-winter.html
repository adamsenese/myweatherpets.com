Best Dog Booties for Snow, Ice, and Salt

Winter is rough on paws. Crusted snow and jagged ice can nick and split paw pads, packed snowballs build up between the toes and chafe, and the rock salt and chemical de-icers spread across sidewalks can sting raw skin and irritate the stomach when your dog licks it off at home. Good booties solve all of that at once: a clean barrier between sensitive pads and a frozen, salted street. Here are the dog booties we would actually put on a dog this winter.

This is not just a comfort call. The American Kennel Club notes that common de-icers like calcium chloride and sodium chloride can irritate and burn paw pads, and that some are toxic if a dog licks them off. If your dog limps, lifts a paw, or stops dead halfway through a cold walk, their pads are usually the reason, and a bootie is the fastest fix.

How we picked

We leaned on editorial gear tests and long-run owner reviews, then weighed each bootie on what decides whether it survives a real winter walk. Fit and security came first, because the most common complaint is that boots fly off in the snow; we favored a genuine closure or a snug pull-on design. Grip mattered next: a textured or lugged sole keeps a dog steady on ice instead of skating. Salt and moisture protection is the whole point, so we looked for waterproof or sealed materials that block slush and de-icer rather than soaking it up. And we weighed sizing, since a bootie only works if it fits. We are not quoting prices or star ratings here, since those drift; we are describing how each one performs.

How to measure your dog's paws

Almost every sizing complaint traces back to a skipped measurement. Have your dog stand so the paw bears weight, set the front paw on a piece of paper, and mark the widest points on both sides. The distance between those marks is the paw width that maps to most bootie size charts. Measure the front paws, which usually run a touch larger than the back, and round up if you land between sizes. Too tight pinches and gets yanked off; too loose spins and falls in the snow.

Getting a dog used to booties

The high-stepping, frozen-marionette walk most dogs do the first time they wear boots is normal, and it passes. Do not strap four boots on and head into a storm. Start indoors: put the booties on for a minute or two, reward calm standing with treats, then take them off. Build up to short indoor walks, then a quick trip outside, and only then a full cold-weather outing. Most dogs stop noticing within a few sessions once the boots become a normal part of the routine.

Booties are not the only line of defense, and they pair well with good habits. The AKC's guidance on dog-safe ice melts and the dangers of common de-icers is worth a read before winter sets in, and even with boots on it is smart to follow basic paw-cleaning habits after walks to wipe away any salt or chemical residue. For a deeper look at the boots themselves, the AKC also covers how to choose dog boots for any weather, including why Velcro straps and grooved anti-slip soles matter.

Jacksonville weather facts every pet owner should know

  • Jacksonville's average highs top 90F in July (91.9F) and hover there in August (90.7F), past the danger line for midday dog walks, so beach trips work best at sunrise.
  • Summer is storm season: June through September each average 6.77 to 7.60 inches of rain, so plan indoor games around the near-daily thunderstorm hours.
  • Even in January, highs average 65.5F, great walking weather, though lows of 42.4F mean thin-coated dogs may want a light sweater at dawn.
  • Jacksonville's normals show no measurable snow, but the city fell to 7F on January 21, 1985, proof that Florida pets can still meet a hard freeze.

Jacksonville pet weather questions

July averages 91.9F, above the 90F danger threshold, and coastal humidity makes it feel worse, so summer walks belong at dawn when lows average 72.7F. From May through September apply the 77F caution and 82F risky rules.
Humid air slows a dog's panting-based cooling, so an 85F Jacksonville afternoon can stress a dog like a much hotter dry day. With September still averaging an 87.1F high, keep the early-walk habit going well into fall.
June is the wettest month at 7.60 inches, and September is close behind at 7.56 inches. Afternoon thunderstorms are near-daily in summer, so schedule walks for mornings, watch for lightning at the beach, and towel dogs off to prevent hot spots.

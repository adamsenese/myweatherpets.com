Raleigh weather facts every pet owner should know

  • Raleigh's July highs average 90.8F, right in the dangerous zone for dog walks, and the city set its all-time record of 106F as recently as July 5, 2024.
  • Humid Carolina summers keep July nights near an average 70.2F, so even sunrise walks call for water and a slower pace for seniors and flat-faced breeds.
  • Snow is scarce at 5.2 inches a year (January leads with 2.6 inches), but Raleigh's occasional ice storms make paw-slicing sidewalks, so booties earn their keep.
  • January lows average 31.8F, meaning small and senior dogs need coats for most winter-morning walks, while midday highs near 51.9F are comfortable for everyone.

Raleigh pet weather questions

June through August, when average highs hit 87.4F to 90.8F, afternoon walks are risky to dangerous, and humidity makes it worse. Go before 8 am, when July mornings start near 70.2F, and test pavement with the back of your hand.
Small, thin-coated, and senior dogs do on most winter mornings, since January lows average 31.8F, below the 45F coat threshold. Midday walks at average highs of 51.9F are usually coat-optional.
Raleigh averages just 5.2 inches of snow a year, but freezing rain is the bigger hazard: ice hides on shaded sidewalks after storms. Wipe paws after walks to remove de-icing salt and check pads for cuts.

Milwaukee weather facts every pet owner should know

  • Milwaukee averages 48.7 inches of snow a year, with about 14.9 inches in January alone, so booties, paw balm, and a good coat are core equipment for city dogs.
  • January lows average 17.2F, below the 20F dangerous threshold, meaning midwinter potty breaks should be quick and small dogs should be layered up.
  • Summers by Lake Michigan are comparatively kind: July highs average 81.9F, just past the cautious threshold, and the lake breeze keeps shoreline walks cooler than inland routes.
  • Milwaukee has still hit 105F (July 24, 1934), so even in this cold-winter city, a hot-day plan of dawn walks and pavement checks belongs in every owner's toolkit.

Milwaukee pet weather questions

January lows average 17.2F, under the 20F dangerous line, so overnight and early-morning outings in midwinter should be minutes, not miles. Between 20F and 32F, which covers typical December through February afternoons (highs 30.9F to 36.1F), keep walks short and coat up small or senior dogs.
With 48.7 inches of average annual snow and January alone bringing about 14.9 inches, salted sidewalks are constant. Use booties or paw balm before walks and rinse paws after, since salt and de-icer cause cracking and are toxic when licked off.
Occasionally. July highs average 81.9F, right at the edge of the risky band, and heat waves have pushed the city to 105F. On hot days walk early, when July lows average 64.7F, and take advantage of Lake Michigan breezes on lakefront routes.

Grand Rapids weather facts every pet owner should know

  • Lake-effect snow off Lake Michigan buries Grand Rapids in 77.6 inches of snow in an average year, so paw booties earn their keep from November through March.
  • January is the snowiest month at 22.6 inches, and with average lows of 18.6F it sits below the 20F danger line for extended dog walks.
  • The record high of 108F was set on July 13, 1936, but typical July days top out near 83.1F, so summer mornings are usually safe for a long sniff walk.
  • Grand Rapids grew up around the rapids of the Grand River, and its riverside paths give city dogs easy access to water views in every season.

Grand Rapids pet weather questions

January in Grand Rapids averages a high of 31.0F and a low of 18.6F, so most winter walks fall in the 20 to 32F caution band. Below 20F, which happens regularly on January and February nights, keep outings to 10 or 15 minutes and add a coat for small or senior dogs.
With 77.6 inches of snow in an average year and 22.6 inches in January alone, salted sidewalks are a constant. Use booties or paw wax, rinse feet after walks, and trim fur between pads so ice balls cannot form.
July averages a high of 83.1F, just past the 82F risky threshold, so midday summer walks deserve caution. Shift to mornings when temperatures are near the 62.5F average July low, and save strenuous fetch for those cooler hours.

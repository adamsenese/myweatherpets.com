Richmond weather facts every pet owner should know

  • July highs in Richmond average 89.5F, right at the edge of the 90F danger zone for dog walks, and Virginia humidity makes it feel even hotter, so summer walks belong at dawn.
  • Richmond's record high is 107F and its record low is -12F, a 119 degree spread, so local dogs need both a hot-weather plan and a cold-snap plan.
  • Snow is a minor player here: Richmond averages just 8.8 inches a year, with January's 3.7 inches the peak, so booties see far more use on hot summer pavement than on ice.
  • Winters are mild by East Coast standards, with January highs averaging 47.8F, which keeps midday winter walks comfortable for most dogs without heavy gear.

Richmond pet weather questions

Treat afternoons cautiously from June through September: July averages an 89.5F high, essentially at the 90F danger threshold, and August is close behind at 87.5F. High humidity makes it worse, so walk before 9 am or after sunset and check pavement temperature with your hand.
Humidity slows a dog's panting-based cooling, so a humid 85F day in Richmond can stress a dog as much as a drier 90F day. With average July lows of 69.2F, even early mornings stay warm, so bring water and watch heavy panting closely in midsummer.
Small, senior, and thin-coated dogs benefit from a coat when temps drop below 45F, which covers most Richmond winter mornings since January lows average 28.8F. Hardy breeds are generally fine, and with only 8.8 inches of average annual snow, ice and salt are occasional rather than daily concerns.

Why a pet weather app?

Because the forecast decides your pet's whole day. It sets when you walk, how long you stay out, whether the pavement is safe for paws, and whether tonight is a thundershirt night. WeatherPets puts your own dog or cat in front of that forecast — real Apple WeatherKit data, severe-weather alerts, and home screen widgets starring the pet you already check on more often than the sky.

It's also why we write. The WeatherPets blog is a growing library of weather-smart pet care, built on primary veterinary sources. Start with the guides owners search for most: what temperature is safe for a dog walk, the warning signs of dog heatstroke, the 7-second pavement test for summer paws, and how cold is too cold for dogs.

WeatherPets, answered

WeatherPets is a free iOS weather app that turns your own dog or cat into your personal weather reporter. You add one photo, and AI places your real pet into weather scenes that match your local forecast, alongside accurate hourly and 10-day forecasts, severe weather alerts, and home screen widgets.
Add a photo of your pet and pick a few personality traits. WeatherPets then generates scenes of your pet dressed for the real weather at your location, and delivers the forecast in their voice through morning and evening reports and home screen widgets.
Yes, WeatherPets is free to download and includes core weather data, one pet profile, and a limited number of AI scenes. A premium subscription unlocks unlimited scene generation, multiple pets, widgets, style packs, and personality-driven notifications.
WeatherPets is powered by Apple Weather (WeatherKit) for accurate current conditions, hourly and 10-day forecasts, and severe weather alerts, the same quality of data as any top weather app.
WeatherPets is available for iPhone running iOS 17 or later, including home screen and lock screen widgets.
Yes. WeatherPets is built around your own pet, whether a dog, cat, or another companion animal. Upload one clear photo and the app generates weather scenes starring that pet.
